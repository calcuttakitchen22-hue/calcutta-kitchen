steps:
  - group: Phase 1 — Preparation
    instruction: Wash the rice thoroughly until the water runs mostly clear and soak it for 2 hours
  - group: Phase 1 — Preparation
    instruction: In a large bowl combine chicken, curd, ginger juice, garlic juice, refined oil, red chilli powder and Kashmiri chilli powder. Mix thoroughly and marinate for 2 hours. Do not add salt at this stage
  - group: Phase 1 — Preparation
    instruction: Thinly slice the onions and keep them ready for making beresta
  - group: Phase 1 — Preparation
    instruction: Peel the potatoes, cut them in half and keep submerged in water to prevent discoloration
  - group: Phase 1 — Preparation
    instruction: Prepare the biryani masala by dry roasting the first-stage spices until fragrant. Add the second-stage spices and salt, roast briefly, cool completely and grind into a fine powder. Sieve and reserve the fine powder
  - group: Phase 1 — Preparation
    instruction: Prepare the aroma mix by combining kewra water, rose water, meetha attar, lemon juice and sugar. Stir until the sugar dissolves
  - group: Phase 2 — Chicken, Potato & Yakhni
    instruction: Fry the sliced onions in refined oil until deep golden brown and crisp. Remove and reserve as beresta
  - group: Phase 2 — Chicken, Potato & Yakhni
    instruction: In a separate pan cook the potatoes with beresta, red chilli powder, Kashmiri chilli powder, salt and a little water until about 70% cooked. Remove the potatoes, reserve the rogan and discard the remaining stock
  - group: Phase 2 — Chicken, Potato & Yakhni
    instruction: In the beresta oil add cardamom, cloves and cinnamon. Once aromatic add the marinated chicken and salt. Cook until the chicken begins releasing moisture
  - group: Phase 2 — Chicken, Potato & Yakhni
    instruction: Add the reserved potato rogan and some beresta. Continue cooking until the chicken is approximately 60–70% cooked and the gravy develops flavour
  - group: Phase 2 — Chicken, Potato & Yakhni
    instruction: Carefully collect the rogan floating on top and reserve it separately. Strain the remaining liquid to obtain a clear yakhni
  - group: Phase 2 — Chicken, Potato & Yakhni
    instruction: Prepare the yakhni mixture by combining the strained yakhni with biryani masala, aroma mix, saffron milk, red chilli powder, Kashmiri chilli powder, prunes and salt to taste
  - group: Phase 2 — Chicken, Potato & Yakhni
    instruction: Warm the reserved rogan with ghee and khoya to create the final rogan-ghee mixture
  - group: Phase 3 — Rice, Layering & Dum
    instruction: Begin layering in the dum handi with the chicken as the bottom layer
  - group: Phase 3 — Rice, Layering & Dum
    instruction: Pour part of the yakhni mixture over the chicken and sprinkle biryani masala evenly
  - group: Phase 3 — Rice, Layering & Dum
    instruction: Add a layer of beresta followed by the potatoes
  - group: Phase 3 — Rice, Layering & Dum
    instruction: Sprinkle more biryani masala, additional beresta, khoya and the prunes
  - group: Phase 3 — Rice, Layering & Dum
    instruction: Bring the rice cooking water to a boil with salt, cardamom, cloves, cinnamon and vinegar. Add the soaked rice and cook until approximately 70% done
  - group: Phase 3 — Rice, Layering & Dum
    instruction: Drain the rice thoroughly and spread it evenly over the layered chicken and potatoes
  - group: Phase 3 — Rice, Layering & Dum
    instruction: Top with the remaining beresta, rogan-ghee mixture and saffron milk mixture
  - group: Phase 3 — Rice, Layering & Dum
    instruction: Seal the handi with dough to prevent steam from escaping
  - group: Phase 3 — Rice, Layering & Dum
    instruction: Cook on high flame for 10 minutes, then reduce to low flame and continue dum cooking for 25 minutes
  - group: Phase 3 — Rice, Layering & Dum
    instruction: Turn off the heat and allow the biryani to rest undisturbed for 15–20 minutes before opening the seal
  - group: Phase 3 — Rice, Layering & Dum
    instruction: Gently fluff the rice, ensuring the layers remain intact, and serve hot
tips:
  - The rice should be only 70% cooked before dum
  - Do not add salt to the chicken marinade
  - Fine biryani masala is essential for smooth flavour distribution
  - Reserve the rogan carefully as it adds colour and richness
  - Resting after dum is crucial for proper flavour development
faq:
  - question: Why are potatoes used in Kolkata biryani?
    answer: Potatoes are a defining characteristic of Kolkata style biryani and absorb the flavour of the yakhni beautifully
  - question: Can I skip meetha attar?
    answer: Yes, but a small quantity adds the traditional aroma
  - question: Why is vinegar added while boiling rice?
    answer: It helps keep the rice grains separate
  - question: Can I make it ahead?
    answer: Yes, biryani tastes even better after resting for a few hours
