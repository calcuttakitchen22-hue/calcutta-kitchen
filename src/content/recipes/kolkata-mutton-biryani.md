about:
  - "Kolkata Mutton Biryani is known for its restrained spice profile, long-grained rice, large tender pieces of mutton and the iconic potato. This version keeps the traditional multi-stage character of the dish while organising the process so that one person can manage it efficiently. The mutton is marinated and bhunaoed separately, the potatoes are cooked on their own, and the cooked mutton liquor is divided into concentrated tar and natural rogan. A strained yakhni, saffron essence milk and rogan-ghee are then used for different purposes during layering before the biryani is sealed and finished on dum."

tips:
  - "Use large bone-in mutton pieces so they stay juicy and intact through pressure cooking and dum"
  - "Do not replace the mutton with chicken using the same measurements; the meat requires a different spice balance, cooking time and moisture strategy"
  - "Remove birista slightly before it reaches the final desired colour because it continues darkening after leaving the oil"
  - "Do not discard the birista oil; use it for the potatoes and then as part of the mutton cooking base"
  - "Cook the potatoes separately to about 90–95% so their doneness does not depend entirely on dum"
  - "Do not put the potatoes into the mutton gravy"
  - "The mutton must be fully tender before layering; dum is not meant to tenderize tough meat"
  - "Keep tar, yakhni, saffron essence milk and rogan-ghee separate because each performs a different job in the final biryani"
  - "Use all the yakhni only on the bottom mutton-and-potato layer"
  - "Do not fully cook the rice before dum; 75–80% is the target"
  - "Keep the final rice patchy white, pale saffron and glossy rather than uniformly yellow"
  - "Serve vertically instead of mixing the whole handi"
faq:
  - question: "Why is the mutton biryani masala different from the chicken biryani masala?"
    answer: "Mutton has a deeper flavour, more fat and a longer cooking time than chicken, so the aromatic balance is adjusted rather than simply reusing the same formula."
  - question: "Why is biryani masala added both in the marinade and later with the birista?"
    answer: "The masala in the marinade builds a deeper base, while the smaller later addition refreshes aromatic notes that would otherwise mellow during long cooking."
  - question: "Why are the potatoes cooked separately?"
    answer: "Cooking them separately lets you control their doneness and keeps their flavour clean instead of coating them in mutton gravy."
  - question: "What are tar and rogan?"
    answer: "Tar is the concentrated meat cooking liquid left after the mutton is cooked, while rogan is the natural rendered mutton fat that rises to the top."
  - question: "Why is the yakhni strained?"
    answer: "Straining removes onion, spice and curd particles so the final yakhni is smooth and does not make the rice gritty."
  - question: "Why does all the yakhni go only on the bottom layer?"
    answer: "It gives the mutton and potato layer concentrated flavour and moisture without soaking the upper rice layers."
  - question: "Can I fully cook the rice before layering?"
    answer: "No. Cook the rice only to about 75–80% so it can finish during dum without becoming mushy."
  - question: "Can I use dum to finish cooking tough mutton?"
    answer: "No. The mutton should already be tender before layering; dum is mainly for finishing the rice and integrating aroma, moisture and fat."
