ingredients:

  - name: Salt to taste
  - name: 8 tbsp mustard oil

  - name: 2 tbsp garlic paste

  - name: 4 cloves

  - name: 1/2 tsp garam masala
  - name: 1/2 tsp sugar
  - name: Water as required
equipment:
  - name: Kadai
  - name: Mixing bowl
steps:
  - instruction: Marinate chicken with curd, turmeric powder and salt for 30 minutes
  - instruction: Heat mustard oil until smoking, then reduce the flame slightly
  - instruction: Add bay leaves, cinnamon, cardamom and cloves
  - instruction: Add chopped onions and a pinch of salt, then cook on medium-low
      heat until deep golden and slightly sticky
  - instruction: Add ginger paste and garlic paste, cook until the raw smell disappears
  - instruction: Add chopped tomatoes if using, add a little sugar and cover for 5
      minutes until softened
  - instruction: Mix all powdered spices with a little water and add to the kadai
  - instruction: Cook until the masala becomes smooth, thick and starts releasing oil
  - instruction: Add the marinated chicken and bhuna on high flame until the chicken
      turns slightly pale and firms up
  - instruction: Lower the flame and continue the koshano process by cooking,
      scraping and adding small splashes of water repeatedly 4-5 times
  - instruction: Add green chillies
  - instruction: Continue cooking until the masala darkens, coats the chicken and
      releases light oil
  - instruction: Cover and cook until the chicken is fully done
  - instruction: Remove the lid and do a final bhuna
  - instruction: Add garam masala and sugar
  - instruction: Rest for 5-10 minutes before serving
tips:
  - The onion stage is critical; cook slowly until deep golden and sticky
  - Always cook powdered spices with a little water to prevent burning
  - Add only small splashes of water during bhuna
  - The koshano process creates the signature texture, not simply reducing gravy
  - A good kosha should have masala clinging to the chicken with slight oil
    release but not be overly oily
faq:
  - question: Can I skip the tomato?
    answer: Yes, increase the curd slightly and the recipe will still work
  - question: Why mix spices with water before adding?
    answer: It prevents burning and helps flavours develop smoothly
  - question: Why is my kosha turning into jhol?
    answer: Too much water during bhuna can loosen the gravy
  - question: Can I make it ahead?
    answer: Yes, the flavour improves after a few hours of resting
