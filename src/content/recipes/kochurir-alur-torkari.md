about:
  - "This Bengali sweet-shop style Motor Alur Tarkari is the familiar potato-and-peas curry served alongside kochuri. The character comes from the complete combination rather than one secret ingredient: skin-on raw potatoes go directly into mustard-oil tempering, followed by ginger and green chilli, whole yellow peas and hot water. As the potatoes cook, their starch naturally gives the jhol body. Black salt, kasuri methi, amchur and a tiny amount of Bengali garam masala are added at the end to complete the salty, spicy, faintly sweet, tangy and aromatic finish."

faq:
  - question: "Why are the potatoes kept unpeeled?"
    answer: "Keeping the skin on is a characteristic of this style of motor alur tarkari."
  - question: "Why shouldn't the potatoes be pre-boiled?"
    answer: "The raw potatoes cook directly in the tempered oil and later release starch into the jhol, helping build its body."
  - question: "Why is the motor boiled separately?"
    answer: "The motor needs to become tender while still holding its shape, so it can be added without becoming mushy."
  - question: "Why does the jhol become slightly starchy?"
    answer: "As the raw potatoes cook in the gravy, they release starch into the liquid, naturally giving the jhol body."
  - question: "Should I mash the potatoes?"
    answer: "Only minimally if needed. Breaking or mashing 1–2 pieces can help the gravy, but too much will make it thick like a potato gravy."
  - question: "When should the black salt and amchur be added?"
    answer: "At the end, after the potatoes are cooked and the gravy has reached the right consistency."
  - question: "How much garam masala should I use?"
    answer: "Only a tiny amount—⅛ tsp. It should support the finish rather than make the curry taste like aloo dum."
  - question: "Can I use rosogolla syrup instead of sugar?"
    answer: "Some reports describe this as a variation in certain sweet shops, but it should not be treated as universal. Regular sugar is sufficient."
  - question: "What should the final consistency be?"
    answer: "Loose and lightly starchy, with enough body to coat the potato lightly but not thick like aloo dum."
  - question: "What should the final flavour taste like?"
    answer: "Salty, spicy, faintly sweet and faintly tangy, with hing and panch-phoron aroma and kasuri methi in the background."
