about:
  - "This Bengali sweet-shop style Hing Dal Kochuri is built around the filling rather than the puff. Urad dal gives the filling its body, hing supplies the distinctive aroma, and a roasted fennel-cumin-dry chilli masala adds the warm spice layer. The key is to use very little water while grinding the dal and then cook the filling only until it becomes soft and cohesive—not dry, sandy or brittle. The dough is rested, the kochuri is rolled to about 10–11 cm and fried around 180°C until lightly golden."

faq:
  - question: "Why is Biulir Dal used for this kochuri?"
    answer: "The urad dal forms the body and texture of the filling, while hing supplies the distinctive aroma associated with hing kochuri."
  - question: "Why should the dal be drained so well?"
    answer: "Extra water makes the ground filling wetter and increases the amount of cooking needed to reach a cohesive texture."
  - question: "Why use so little water while grinding?"
    answer: "The goal is a fine but not completely pasty grind. Too much water makes the filling harder to cook down and changes its texture."
  - question: "Should the filling be completely dry?"
    answer: "No. It should be cooked and cohesive, but still soft. A hard, crumbly or sandy filling is over-dried."
  - question: "Why is hing added before the ground dal?"
    answer: "Hing is bloomed briefly in the hot oil so its aroma can spread through the filling before the dal is added."
  - question: "Why is the roasted masala made separately?"
    answer: "Dry-roasting the fennel, cumin and dry chilli builds a warm aromatic spice layer that is added to the hing-led filling."
  - question: "Why must the filling cool before stuffing?"
    answer: "A hot filling can soften the dough and make shaping and sealing more difficult."
  - question: "How thin should the kochuri be rolled?"
    answer: "About 10–11 cm in diameter, but not paper-thin."
  - question: "What temperature should the oil be?"
    answer: "Around 180°C. Gently pressing the kochuri as it enters the oil can help encourage puffing."
  - question: "What is the main texture target for the filling?"
    answer: "Cooked and cohesive, with enough moisture to stay soft rather than becoming sandy or brittle."
