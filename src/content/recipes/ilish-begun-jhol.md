about:
  - "Ilish Begun Jhol is a light Bengali fish curry where the delicate flavour of hilsa is paired with soft brinjal in a simple kalonji and green chilli broth. The key is timing: the begun is partially fried and cooked in the jhol first, while the lightly fried Ilish is added only towards the end. This keeps the brinjal tender without overcooking the delicate fish."

steps:
  - group: "Marinate the Fish"
    instruction: "Gently coat the hilsa pieces with 1 tsp salt and ½ tsp turmeric powder"
  - group: "Marinate the Fish"
    instruction: "Marinate for 10–15 minutes"
  - group: "Prepare the Begun"
    instruction: "Cut the brinjal lengthwise into moderately thick pieces"
  - group: "Prepare the Begun"
    instruction: "Coat with ¼ tsp salt and ¼ tsp turmeric powder"
  - group: "Prepare the Begun"
    instruction: "Rest for 5–10 minutes"
  - group: "Prepare the Begun"
    instruction: "Keep the pieces fairly large so they do not disintegrate while cooking"
  - group: "Fry the Begun"
    instruction: "Heat the mustard oil until smoking, then reduce the heat slightly"
  - group: "Fry the Begun"
    instruction: "Add the marinated begun and fry until lightly golden outside and partially cooked inside"
  - group: "Fry the Begun"
    instruction: "Keep the begun firm enough to hold its shape and do not cook it completely"
  - group: "Fry the Begun"
    instruction: "Remove from the oil and keep aside"
  - group: "Lightly Fry the Ilish"
    instruction: "In the same mustard oil, carefully add the marinated hilsa pieces"
  - group: "Lightly Fry the Ilish"
    instruction: "Fry for only 20–30 seconds on each side"
  - group: "Lightly Fry the Ilish"
    instruction: "Remove immediately without cooking or crisping the fish completely"
  - group: "Temper the Oil"
    instruction: "In the same oil, add ½ tsp kalonji and 2–3 slit green chillies"
  - group: "Temper the Oil"
    instruction: "Let the kalonji crackle and the green chillies flavour the oil"
  - group: "Make the Jhol"
    instruction: "Reduce the heat and add ½ tsp turmeric powder"
  - group: "Make the Jhol"
    instruction: "Immediately add a small splash of hot water to prevent the turmeric from burning"
  - group: "Make the Jhol"
    instruction: "Pour in 2–2½ cups hot water"
  - group: "Make the Jhol"
    instruction: "Add salt to taste and ½ tsp sugar"
  - group: "Make the Jhol"
    instruction: "Bring the jhol to a proper boil and keep the consistency fairly thin"
  - group: "Cook the Begun"
    instruction: "Add the fried begun to the boiling jhol"
  - group: "Cook the Begun"
    instruction: "Cover and cook over medium-low heat for 3–4 minutes"
  - group: "Cook the Begun"
    instruction: "Cook until the begun is almost completely tender while still holding its shape"
  - group: "Add the Ilish"
    instruction: "Carefully arrange the lightly fried hilsa pieces in the jhol"
  - group: "Add the Ilish"
    instruction: "Avoid stirring with a spatula once the fish has been added"
  - group: "Add the Ilish"
    instruction: "Gently shake the kadai if you need to redistribute the jhol"
  - group: "Add the Ilish"
    instruction: "Cover and cook over low heat for 5–6 minutes without vigorously boiling the fish"
  - group: "Finish"
    instruction: "Add the remaining 2–3 fresh slit green chillies"
  - group: "Finish"
    instruction: "Add 1 tsp raw mustard oil"
  - group: "Finish"
    instruction: "Switch off the heat"
  - group: "Finish"
    instruction: "Cover and rest for 5 minutes before serving"
  - group: "Finish"
    instruction: "Serve with hot steamed rice"
tips:
  - "The begun and Ilish should not be added to the jhol at the same time because begun needs longer to cook"
  - "Keep the begun pieces fairly large so they remain intact in the jhol"
  - "Do not fully cook the begun while frying; it should finish cooking in the jhol"
  - "Fry the Ilish for only 20–30 seconds per side to lightly set the exterior without cooking it through"
  - "Use the same mustard oil for frying and tempering to retain the flavour released by the begun and Ilish"
  - "Add a splash of hot water immediately after adding turmeric to prevent it from burning"
  - "Keep the jhol thin; Ilish Begun Jhol is not meant to have a thick gravy"
  - "Cook the begun in the jhol before adding the Ilish"
  - "Avoid stirring with a spatula after adding the Ilish because the delicate fish can break"
  - "Do not vigorously boil the jhol after adding the fish"
  - "Finish with raw mustard oil and fresh green chillies for aroma"
  - "Rest the finished jhol for 5 minutes before serving"
faq:
  - question: "Why should the begun be cooked before adding the Ilish?"
    answer: "Begun takes longer to become tender than hilsa. Cooking it first allows the brinjal to soften properly without overcooking the delicate fish."
  - question: "Why is the Ilish fried for only 20–30 seconds per side?"
    answer: "The brief frying only sets the exterior of the fish. The Ilish finishes cooking gently in the jhol, which helps keep it soft and delicate."
  - question: "Should I fully fry the begun before adding it to the jhol?"
    answer: "No. Fry it only until lightly golden and partially cooked. It should finish cooking in the jhol."
  - question: "Why shouldn't I stir after adding the Ilish?"
    answer: "Hilsa becomes delicate as it cooks and can easily break. Gently shaking the kadai distributes the jhol without damaging the fish."
  - question: "Should I make the gravy thick?"
    answer: "No. Ilish Begun Jhol is traditionally a light, thin jhol rather than a thick gravy."
  - question: "Why add raw mustard oil at the end?"
    answer: "A small amount of raw mustard oil adds the characteristic sharp mustard aroma to the finished jhol."
