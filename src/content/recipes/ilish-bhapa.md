steps:
  - group: "Phase 1 — Fish Preparation"
    instruction: "Clean and pat the hilsa dry"
  - group: "Phase 1 — Fish Preparation"
    instruction: "Marinate with salt, turmeric and mustard oil"
  - group: "Phase 1 — Fish Preparation"
    instruction: "Rest for 15–20 minutes"
  - group: "Phase 2 — Mustard Paste"
    instruction: "Soak the yellow and black mustard seeds in cold water for 15–20 minutes and drain completely"
  - group: "Phase 2 — Mustard Paste"
    instruction: "Grind the soaked mustard seeds with cashews, green chilli and ice cubes (or cold water) until smooth"
  - group: "Phase 2 — Mustard Paste"
    instruction: "Add the grated coconut and grind briefly until incorporated"
  - group: "Phase 2 — Mustard Paste"
    instruction: "Do not strain the paste"
  - group: "Phase 3 — Marinade"
    instruction: "Mix the mustard paste with curd, sugar and mustard oil until smooth"
  - group: "Phase 3 — Marinade"
    instruction: "Taste and adjust seasoning if required"
  - group: "Phase 3 — Marinade"
    instruction: "Coat the marinated fish evenly"
  - group: "Phase 4 — Steaming"
    instruction: "Spread a thin layer of mustard paste in a steel container"
  - group: "Phase 4 — Steaming"
    instruction: "Arrange the fish pieces and cover with the remaining paste"
  - group: "Phase 4 — Steaming"
    instruction: "Top with slit green chillies and mustard oil"
  - group: "Phase 4 — Steaming"
    instruction: "Seal tightly and steam over medium heat for 20 minutes"
  - group: "Phase 4 — Steaming"
    instruction: "Rest for 5 minutes before opening and serving"
tips:
  - "Soak mustard seeds before grinding for a smoother paste"
  - "Grind only until smooth to prevent bitterness"
  - "Use ice cubes or cold water to keep the paste cool while grinding"
  - "Do not strain the mustard paste if using a good mixer"
  - "Rest the fish for 5 minutes after steaming before serving"
faq:
  - question: "Why does mustard taste bitter?"
    answer: "Usually due to over-grinding, overheating during grinding or old mustard seeds"
  - question: "Why soak the mustard?"
    answer: "Soaking softens the seeds so they grind faster and more smoothly"
  - question: "Why add coconut?"
    answer: "Coconut balances the sharpness of mustard and gives the gravy a richer texture"
  - question: "How do I know the fish is cooked?"
    answer: "It should be opaque, flake easily and separate cleanly from the bone"
  - question: "Why is my gravy watery?"
    answer: "Too much water while grinding, a loose lid during steaming or condensation entering the container can thin the gravy"
