steps:
  - instruction: Marinate the hilsa with salt, turmeric and mustard oil for 10–15 minutes
  - instruction: Heat mustard oil until smoking and lightly fry the fish for 20–30
      seconds per side
  - instruction: Remove immediately without cooking through
  - instruction: Soak the white and black mustard seeds for 20–30 minutes and drain
      completely
  - instruction: Grind with green chillies and ice cubes using short pulses until smooth
  - instruction: Strain the paste through a fine sieve
  - instruction: Use the mustard residue with a little water to grind the poppy seeds
  - instruction: Mix the poppy seed paste back into the strained mustard paste
  - instruction: Mix the mustard-posto paste with turmeric, salt, warm water and
      mustard oil until smooth
  - instruction: Temper the remaining mustard oil with kalonji and slit green chillies
  - instruction: Pour in the mustard gravy and cook over low heat for 2–3 minutes
      without boiling vigorously
  - instruction: Arrange the fried fish carefully
  - instruction: Cover and cook for 6–8 minutes
  - instruction: Finish with raw mustard oil and fresh slit green chillies
  - instruction: Rest for 5 minutes before serving
tips:
  - Always use ice cubes while grinding mustard to prevent bitterness
  - Grind using short pulses instead of continuous grinding
  - Strain the mustard paste for a silky gravy
  - Lightly fry the fish without cooking it through
  - Never boil the mustard gravy vigorously after adding the fish
  - Rest the curry for 5 minutes before serving
faq:
  - question: Why use both white and black mustard?
    answer: White mustard provides body while black mustard adds sharp flavour
  - question: Why add poppy seeds?
    answer: Posto softens the pungency of mustard and creates a creamier gravy
  - question: Why use ice cubes while grinding?
    answer: They keep the mustard cool and reduce bitterness
  - question: Why fry the fish first?
    answer: Light frying helps the fish retain its shape during cooking
  - question: Why should the gravy not boil vigorously?
    answer: Excessive boiling can make the mustard bitter and overcook the hilsa
