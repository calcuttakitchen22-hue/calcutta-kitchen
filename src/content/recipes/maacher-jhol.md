ingredients:
  - name: 6 pieces Rui fish (700-800 g)
  - name: Salt to taste
  - name: 1.5 tsp turmeric powder
  - name: 2 medium potatoes, cut lengthwise
  - name: 1 small tomato
  - name: 3-4 green chillies, slit
  - name: 1/2 tsp kalonji
  - name: 1/2 tsp red chilli powder
  - name: 1 tsp cumin powder
  - name: 5 tbsp mustard oil
  - name: 3.5-4 cups hot water
equipment:
  - name: Kadai
  - name: Fish spatula
steps:
  - instruction: Marinate the fish with salt and turmeric and rest for 15-20 minutes
  - instruction: Heat mustard oil until smoking and lightly fry the fish until golden
  - instruction: Remove and keep aside
  - instruction: Fry the potatoes in the same oil with a little salt and turmeric
      until lightly golden
  - instruction: Add kalonji and green chillies
  - instruction: Add turmeric, red chilli powder, and cumin powder
  - instruction: Immediately add a splash of water and cook until the spices bubble smoothly
  - instruction: Add tomato and cook until softened
  - instruction: Pour in hot water and bring to a simmer
  - instruction: Add the potatoes and cook for 6-8 minutes
  - instruction: Gently add the fish pieces and simmer for 4-5 minutes
  - instruction: Avoid excessive stirring to prevent the fish from breaking
  - instruction: Cook until the jhol is thin, light, and slightly glossy
  - instruction: Serve hot with steamed rice
tips:
  - Do not over-marinate the fish as it can mask the fresh fish flavor
  - Lightly fry the fish, do not make it crispy
  - Add water immediately after the powdered spices to prevent burning
  - This is a light jhol, not a kosha, so avoid aggressively frying the spices
  - Judge the consistency visually rather than relying only on measurements
  - Simmer gently after adding the fish to keep the pieces intact
faq:
  - question: Why shouldn't the fish be fried until crispy?
    answer: The fish continues cooking in the jhol and should remain soft and tender
  - question: Can I skip the tomato?
    answer: Yes, the tomato is optional and is used only to round off the flavor
  - question: Why add water immediately after the spices?
    answer: It prevents the spices from burning and keeps the jhol light
  - question: How thin should the jhol be?
    answer: It should mix beautifully with rice but still retain enough body and flavor
