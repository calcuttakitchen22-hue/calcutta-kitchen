tips:
  - "Use large mutton pieces of around 60 g each"
  - "Raw papaya with skin helps make the meat significantly more tender"
  - "Grated onions create a smoother kosha texture than chopped onions"
  - "Slice the frying onions across the grain so they break down faster"
  - "Whole Kashmiri chilies give a richer natural red colour than chilli powder"
  - "Never rush Mutton Kosha; its flavour develops through slow cooking"
  - "The signature colour comes from patient browning, sugar caramelisation and slow cooking, not food colour"
  - "Biryani masala can be used instead of garam masala for a deeper aroma"
  - "Serve with Basanti Pulao, luchi, paratha or steamed rice"
faq:
  - question: "Why should I use raw papaya in the marinade?"
    answer: "Raw papaya helps tenderize the mutton during marination, making it suitable for slow cooking."
  - question: "Why shouldn't I add salt while frying the onions?"
    answer: "Salt draws moisture from the onions and can slow down the browning needed to develop the deep colour of Mutton Kosha."
  - question: "Can I use biryani masala instead of garam masala?"
    answer: "Yes. Biryani masala can be used for a deeper and more aromatic finish."
  - question: "What should I do if the bottom starts sticking?"
    answer: "If the sticking is brown, add a little hot water and scrape the browned bits into the gravy. If it is black or burnt, transfer the curry to another pan without scraping the burnt layer."
  - question: "Can I add more gravy?"
    answer: "Yes. Add ½ cup whisked curd when the mutton is around 80% cooked and continue slow cooking."
