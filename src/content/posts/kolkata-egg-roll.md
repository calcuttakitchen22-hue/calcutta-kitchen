## A Bengali Comfort Classic

Kolkata Egg Roll is one of the city's most iconic street foods. While it appears simple, the perfect roll depends on dozens of small techniques—from building flaky laccha layers and resting the dough to using sugar for browning, cooking on a dry tawa before frying, and mastering the classic egg flip. This recipe explains not just how to make it, but why every step matters.


* Prep Time: 45 mins
* Cook Time: 30 mins
* Serves: 4

## Ingredients


### Dough (4 Rolls)

- 300 g maida
- 1 tsp salt
- 1 tsp sugar
- 2 tbsp ghee
- 170–190 ml warm water

### Layering Mix

- 3 tbsp ghee
- 2 tbsp maida

### Egg Mixture (Per Roll)

- 2 eggs
- Pinch salt
- Pinch white pepper

### Assembly

- Onion (as required)
- Cucumber (as required)
- Green chilli, finely chopped
- Lemon juice
- Chaat masala
- Black salt
- Kasundi
- Green chilli sauce
- Tomato ketchup

## Method


## Dough Preparation

1. Mix maida, salt, sugar and ghee
2. Gradually add warm water and knead into a smooth dough
3. Rest for 30 minutes

## Layer Creation

4. Roll the dough into a circle
5. Cut from the edge to the centre
6. Apply ghee and dry maida evenly
7. Roll into a cone
8. Press into a dough ball
9. Rest again for 15–20 minutes

## Rolling

10. Roll into a medium-thin laccha paratha

## Dry Roast

11. Cook both sides briefly on a dry tawa to remove excess moisture

## Frying

12. Add oil and fry until golden brown and flaky

## Egg Layer

13. Spread beaten eggs on the tawa
14. Season with salt and white pepper
15. Place the paratha over the egg
16. Perform the traditional Kolkata egg flip
17. Cook until the egg is fully set

## Assembly

18. Add onion, cucumber and chopped green chilli
19. Season with chaat masala and black salt
20. Squeeze lemon juice
21. Apply kasundi, green chilli sauce and tomato ketchup

## Wrap

22. Roll tightly
23. Wrap in paper
24. Serve immediately

## Chef's Tips

- Sugar helps balance flavour and improves browning
- Warm water produces a softer dough
- Always rest the dough before rolling
- The cone method creates beautiful flaky layers
- Dry-roast the paratha before frying to remove excess moisture and avoid a raw flour smell
- Do not overfill the roll to maintain proper balance

## Frequently Asked Questions

- Why is sugar added to the dough? It balances flavour and helps develop the classic golden colour during frying
- Why use warm water? It hydrates the flour better and creates a softer dough
- Why dry-roast before frying? It allows excess moisture to escape, preventing a raw flour taste
- Can I use oil instead of ghee? Yes, but ghee gives better flavour and flakier layers
