## A Bengali Comfort Classic

Ilish Bhapa is one of Bengal's most iconic hilsa preparations. The key to a perfect Bhapa lies in preparing a balanced mustard paste, avoiding bitterness while grinding, and steaming the fish just long enough to keep it silky, moist and full of its natural oils.


* Prep Time: 20 mins
* Cook Time: 20 mins
* Serves: 2

## Ingredients


### Fish Marination

- 4 pieces Hilsa (550–650 g)
- 1 tsp Salt
- ½ tsp Turmeric Powder
- 1 tbsp Mustard Oil

### Mustard Paste

- 2 tbsp Yellow Mustard Seeds
- 2 tbsp Black Mustard Seeds
- 5–6 Cashews
- 2 tbsp Freshly Grated Coconut
- 1 Green Chilli
- 2–3 Ice Cubes or Cold Water

### Final Marinade

- 1 tbsp Thick Curd
- 1½ tsp Sugar
- 3 tbsp Mustard Oil

### For Steaming

- 4–5 Slit Green Chillies
- 1 tbsp Mustard Oil

## Method


## Phase 1 — Fish Preparation

1. Clean and pat the hilsa dry
2. Marinate with salt, turmeric and mustard oil
3. Rest for 15–20 minutes

## Phase 2 — Mustard Paste

4. Soak the yellow and black mustard seeds in cold water for 15–20 minutes and drain completely
5. Grind the soaked mustard seeds with cashews, green chilli and ice cubes (or cold water) until smooth
6. Add the grated coconut and grind briefly until incorporated
7. Do not strain the paste

## Phase 3 — Marinade

8. Mix the mustard paste with curd, sugar and mustard oil until smooth
9. Taste and adjust seasoning if required
10. Coat the marinated fish evenly

## Phase 4 — Steaming

11. Spread a thin layer of mustard paste in a steel container
12. Arrange the fish pieces and cover with the remaining paste
13. Top with slit green chillies and mustard oil
14. Seal tightly and steam over medium heat for 20 minutes
15. Rest for 5 minutes before opening and serving

## Chef's Tips

- Soak mustard seeds before grinding for a smoother paste
- Grind only until smooth to prevent bitterness
- Use ice cubes or cold water to keep the paste cool while grinding
- Do not strain the mustard paste if using a good mixer
- Rest the fish for 5 minutes after steaming before serving

## Frequently Asked Questions

- Why does mustard taste bitter? Usually due to over-grinding, overheating during grinding or old mustard seeds
- Why soak the mustard? Soaking softens the seeds so they grind faster and more smoothly
- Why add coconut? Coconut balances the sharpness of mustard and gives the gravy a richer texture
- How do I know the fish is cooked? It should be opaque, flake easily and separate cleanly from the bone
- Why is my gravy watery? Too much water while grinding, a loose lid during steaming or condensation entering the container can thin the gravy
